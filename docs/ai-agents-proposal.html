Stack · Design proposal

Agents for every part of the job search

Five AI agents for Jobs, Resume, Applications, Network, and Prep, built on one shared memory and one approval flow. The agents find, read, draft, and prepare. You decide what leaves your phone.

The short version

Three design principles

1

One agent layer, five specialties

The agents share one memory: your goals, resume versions, jobs, contacts, and applications. Stack's graph already stores all of it. Each agent is a set of tools plus a focused prompt, not a separate bot.

2

Draft first, you approve

Agents prepare messages, applications, and profile edits. Nothing is sent or submitted without your tap. That keeps Stack's existing promise and keeps your LinkedIn and job-board accounts safe.

3

Evidence, not guesses

Like the matcher, agents quote the listing, email, or resume line behind each claim and mark what's unknown. AI-inferred facts are labeled as AI-inferred.

✓

Recommendation: build it, value first. Start with the Jobs fit read and resume tailoring, which are high value and low risk. Add email-driven application tracking and interview prep next. Leave assisted applying for last, and leave autonomous LinkedIn activity out entirely.

The agents

What each agent does, and where the limits are

Jobs agent

Reads postings closely and prepares applications
Fit read: low risk

Works well

  • Reads the full listing and your resume, then explains fit and gaps.
  • Fills in facts the matcher marked "not stated," quoting the text.
  • Prefills Greenhouse, Lever, and Ashby forms, whose layouts are standardized.

Constraints

  • The matcher's requirement checks stay the source of truth. The AI adds evidence and never silently overrides them.
  • Runs on demand or on your top matches, to control cost.
  • Workday needs per-employer accounts; CAPTCHAs block automation; some employers reject automated submissions.
  • Browser automation runs on the Mac. The phone can't drive a browser.

Resume agent

Tailors your resume to each listing, in your own voice
Low risk

Works well

  • Rewrites and reorders bullets toward what the listing emphasizes.
  • Learns your voice from your own writing samples, so edits don't read as generic AI text.
  • Shows a side-by-side diff; your original PDF is never modified.

Constraints

  • It may only rephrase and reorder what's true. It never invents experience or metrics.
  • There is no real universal "ATS score." Applicant tracking systems mostly parse resumes and let recruiters search, so any score is a heuristic and is labeled that way.

Applications agent

Keeps every application's status current, automatically
Medium: email access

Works well

  • Most tracking is ordinary app logic. The AI part is reading your inbox.
  • Detects confirmations, rejections, assessments, and interview invites, then updates status.
  • Adds interviews to your calendar and schedules follow-ups.

Constraints

  • Needs Gmail or Outlook access and your consent for what it reads.
  • Proposes a status change before applying it, with the email quoted.

Network agent

Profile review, finding the right people, and follow-through
Medium: platform rules

Works well

  • Reviews your profile against the goals you set.
  • Plans who to reach: alumni, second-degree connections, the hiring team at companies you applied to.
  • Drafts personalized notes and tracks replies, calls, and follow-ups, building on Stack's contacts and reminders.

Constraints

  • LinkedIn's terms prohibit scraping and automation, and there's no public API for reading others' profiles or sending messages. Use your own data export, pasted text, or a profile PDF.
  • You run searches in your own logged-in browser; the agent analyzes pages you share.
  • Email guessing and mass outreach raise privacy, spam-law, and account risks. Reddit and X APIs are paid or limited.

Prep agent

Practice interviews that score your answers and show how to improve
Low to medium

Three tiers, built in order

1 · Timed questionsTyped answers, a timer, and rubric scoring for technical and behavioral questions.
2 · Spoken answersOn-device transcription, scored on structure, specifics, filler words, and pacing.
3 · Live interviewerA voice conversation: speech-to-text → Claude → text-to-speech, about 1–2 s per turn.

Constraints

  • Claude's API takes text in and gives text out, so voice needs separate speech-to-text and text-to-speech steps.
  • As far as I know, Wispr Flow has no integration API. Open alternatives: Apple's on-device Speech framework, WhisperKit, whisper.cpp.
  • A video avatar costs a lot for little benefit. Build it last, if at all.

Value versus risk

Where each capability sits. This is a judgment, not a measurement: risk covers cost, platform terms, privacy, and how brittle the implementation would be.

Powering the agents

Subscriptions or API keys

Put one provider interface under all the agents, with adapters for the Anthropic API, the OpenAI API, and a local CLI bridge. Choosing a subscription or API keys then becomes a setting, not a rewrite.

If Stack is just for you

Bridge to your own subscription

The Mac server can call your own logged-in CLIs, claude -p or codex exec, using your existing plan.

  • Simplest to start; no API billing.
  • Only works where you're logged in, which is your Mac.
  • Personal use only.
If other people will use Stack

Use API keys

Theirs (bring your own key) or yours. Anthropic's Agent SDK documentation says third-party products shouldn't offer claude.ai login or subscription limits without approval. Check OpenAI's terms for Codex similarly.

  • Choose the model per agent.
  • Batch processing roughly halves the cost of overnight listing scans.
  • A consent screen shows what each agent sends: resume, contacts, email excerpts.
Roadmap

Suggested order: value first, risk last

PHASE 1

Jobs fit read

Builds directly on the matcher; read-only, low cost, immediately useful.

PHASE 2

Resume tailoring

Truthful edits with a diff, plus the parse and keyword check.

PHASE 3

Email tracking

Applications update themselves from replies in your inbox.

PHASE 4

Interview prep

Typed and timed first, then spoken answers, then a live interviewer.

PHASE 5

Network drafting

Referral plans, drafts you send, and follow-up tracking.

PHASE 6

Assisted apply

Prefill Greenhouse, Lever, and Ashby; you review and submit.

Deliberately out of scope

  • Autonomous LinkedIn activity: scraping, auto-connecting, or auto-messaging.
  • Mass outreach or sending anything without your review.
  • Submitting applications without a final human confirmation.
  • Inventing experience, metrics, or credentials on a resume.
